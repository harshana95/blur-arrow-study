Draw where things moved

Each photo was taken with a long exposure, so anything that moved left a blur. The clip beside the photo shows what really happened during the exposure.

  1. Draw one arrow on each part of the picture that moved, in the direction it moved: each moving object, and the background if the camera moved. Press where it starts, drag the way it goes, let go.
  2. Draw quickly, the way you would in a photo app. Do not trace the motion exactly. Up to 5 arrows per photo.
  3. If nothing seems to move, or you cannot tell, press Nothing I can tell.
  4. Press Save and next. Your arrows are saved after every photo, so you can stop and come back.

26 photos, about 20 minutes, plus 21 optional ones at the end if you have time; you can stop at any point and come back later. Please do not look at anyone else's arrows. What is stored: your arrows, the time you drew them, a random id kept in this browser and the initials you type below (optional); no name, email or address. They are used only to test arrow-controlled video generation. They are sent to the researchers each time you press Save and next.
